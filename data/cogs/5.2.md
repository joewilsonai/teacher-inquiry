## Acting on
A standing arrangement she runs: how time, space, transitions, or activities are set up.

## Definition
The arrangements that are in place before anyone does anything: how time is used inside her room, how the room is laid out and who sits where, the routines for moving from one thing to the next, and how an activity is built and how long it runs. The starting place is the arrangement itself, which she sets up and can change, rather than any one student's conduct inside it.

## Fallback meaning
the starting place is how the time, the space, the transition or the activity is set up

## Examples
- Lining up for lunch takes us forever.
- After recess it's fifteen minutes before we can start anything.
- Everyone crowds the supply shelf at once.
- I can't get to the back of the room because the desks are packed in.
- Cleanup takes longer than the activity did.
- The first ten minutes of class disappear every day.
- My rotation is a mess. Nobody knows where to go next.
- The lesson runs long and it falls apart around minute twenty-five.

## Near-misses
- "During work time they keep getting up and talking." This is Management, Motivation & Engagement: they are already in the activity, and she is acting on what they are doing, not on how the activity is set up.
- "Lining up turns into name-calling between two groups of kids." If she puts the name-calling first, this is Culture. If she puts the line first, it is this cog. Go with what she emphasizes.
