## Acting on
Something students are doing, or not doing, that keeps pulling a response from her.

## Definition
What students are doing in the moment and what she does about it: calling out, wandering, refusing, checking out, not starting, not taking up the work. Motivation and engagement belong here too, because whether students take up the work shows up as conduct she responds to. The starting place is the conduct and her response to it, rather than the schedule or layout around it, and rather than the relationships underneath it.

## Fallback meaning
the starting place is the student conduct that keeps pulling a response from you

## Examples
- I say sit down six times before anyone does.
- Half the class puts their heads down the minute I hand out the worksheet.
- A few kids blurt out answers before anyone else gets a chance.
- She won't start, and just stares at me when I ask.
- They're out of their seats sharpening pencils and visiting friends when they should be working.
- They do the first problem and then quit.
- He argues with me every time I ask him to do anything.
- Nobody raises a hand. They sit there and wait for me to give the answer.

## Near-misses
- "It takes forever to get everyone lined up." This is Time, Space, Transitions & Activities: she is acting on a routine she set up, not on what a student is doing.
- "The kids are mean to each other and nobody feels like they belong." This is Culture: she names the climate itself as the thing.
