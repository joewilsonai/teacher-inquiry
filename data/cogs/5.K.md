## Cite sentence
Missouri's Standard 5 has no Quality Indicator for this. Instructional Coordination is a proposed part of this model.

## Acting on
Two demands at the same moment, with teaching as one of them.

## Definition
The moments when she is teaching (a small group, a lesson at the board, a conference with one student) and something else needs her at the same time: the rest of the room, a second group, a student at her elbow. The starting place is the holding of both at once, rather than the conduct of the students she is not with, and rather than the layout of the room.

## Fallback meaning
the starting place is holding your teaching and the rest of the room at the same moment

## Her teaching is named when
- Her own words name teaching of hers at the moment she describes: a lesson she is giving, a group she is working with, a conference with one student about their work.
- It is also named when she says a group or a lesson of hers is waiting on her while she deals with something else.
- Being with one student for something that is not teaching (a shoe, a zipper, an upset child, a trip to the nurse, a login) is not teaching. Neither is a job such as attendance, handing out papers or a phone call.
- A lesson, or a kind of lesson, named only as the time something happens ("during math", "during reading groups", "during conferences") does not name her teaching, unless she also says where she is or that both were on her.
- Centers, stations, independent work or a subject never mean, without her saying so, that she is teaching someone at that moment.

## Counts alongside her teaching when
- Her own words put it on her at the same moment as that teaching, and the teaching is aimed somewhere else. She is with a small group, in a conference with one student, or at the board with part of the class, and the thing is happening with students she is not teaching at that moment. Or she is dealing with the thing while a group or a lesson waits on her.
- It also counts when she says in her own words that the two were on her at once ("at the same time", "while I'm also", "I can't do both").
- It does not have to be going badly. If she only says where she is and what else is going on, that is enough.
- It does not count when a lesson is only the time it happens ("during math", "all through read-aloud") and the students she describes are the ones in that lesson.
- It does not count when she says her teaching is going fine, or names something else as the hard part.

## Examples
- I can't confer with one student without three others lining up at my elbow.
- When I'm working with my small group, the kids working on their own stop.
- I'm trying to teach the lesson and deal with the kid who just walked in late, both at once.
- If I stop to help one table, I lose the other five.
- As soon as I turn to write on the board the back half drifts, and I can't do both.
- The second I sit down with my group, the rest of the room gets loud.
- While I get one group started, the group I just left stalls.
- While I'm sorting out the argument at the sink, my math group sits at the table waiting for me.

## Near-misses
- "Switching stations eats most of our time." No teaching of hers is named.
- "The ones working on their own drift off." She does not say she is teaching anyone else at that moment.
- "While I'm buttoning one child's coat, the rest of the line comes apart." Two things are on her and neither is teaching.
- "During reading groups the room gets noisy." Reading groups are only the time it happens. She does not say she is with a group.
- "Two of them talk through the whole lesson and I keep having to stop." The lesson is when it happens, and the students she describes are in it.
- "My small groups are my favorite part of the day." Her teaching is named and nothing else is on her.
- "My reading group is fine. What wears on me is how the others talk to each other." She says her teaching is fine and names something else as the hard part.
