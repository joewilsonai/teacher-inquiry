## Definition
Is the task the right length, level, and interest to hold them? The starting point here is whether the work fits the students in front of you.

## Indicators
- Some students finish early with nothing to do next
- Students can't get started without help
- The task runs longer than students can sustain attention
- Students call the work boring, pointless, or too hard

## Grade bands
### K-2
Tasks longer than 10 to 15 minutes usually outlast attention. Hands-on and short-cycle work holds better.
### 3-5
Students need a clear "what to do when you're done" and work that feels doable.
### 6-8
Relevance and choice drive effort. Students disengage from work that feels like busywork.

## Examples
- Half of them finish in five minutes and the rest can't start.
- They say it's boring and quit.
- It's too long for them to stay with it.

## Near-misses
- "They don't know what they're supposed to do." This is 5.1.1: the expectation isn't clear.
