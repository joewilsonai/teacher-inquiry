## Definition
This is about how students are engaging with the expectations and with the learning itself: whether they know what they're supposed to be doing, whether the work is holding them, and how behavior gets redirected when it drifts.

## Indicators
- Students off task during work time: talking, wandering, not starting
- The teacher repeats the same redirection many times
- Students say the work is boring or too hard, or announce "I'm done" fast and then drift
- Expectations for an activity are unclear or enforced unevenly
- Low participation, checked-out students, compliance without effort
- A few students' behavior keeps pulling attention away from learning

## Grade bands
### K-2
Attention spans are short, often 10 to 15 minutes. Expectations have to be modeled and practiced, not just stated. Getting up and talking during independent work usually means the expectation or the task length doesn't fit yet. Motivation is tied closely to the teacher's attention and immediate feedback.
### 3-5
Students can hold expectations longer and start comparing fairness ("why does he get to..."). Engagement depends more on whether the task feels doable and meaningful. Social talk with peers competes with the work.
### 6-8
Peer approval drives a lot of behavior, and public redirection can backfire. Engagement hinges on relevance, choice, and autonomy. Apathy ("why do we have to do this") and quiet disengagement are as common as disruption.

## Examples
- During independent work my kids keep getting up and talking and I have to redirect them over and over.
- Half the class finishes in five minutes and then starts goofing off.
- They just won't start the assignment. They sit there until I come over.
- I have three boys who constantly call out during the lesson.
- My 7th graders put their heads down and do nothing during reading.
- When I ask a question nobody raises their hand.
- They follow the rules for me but fall apart with a sub.
- Everything I say I have to say three times.
- Students say the work is boring and stop trying.
- During centers they play instead of doing the task.

## Near-misses
- "It takes my class ten minutes to get from the carpet to their seats." This is 5.2: the problem is moving between activities, not engagement with the work.
- "There's no room for kids to work in groups without bumping into each other." This is 5.2: it's about the physical space.
- "My students are mean to each other and won't work with certain kids." This is 5.3: it's about relationships and belonging, not engagement with the task.
- "They don't seem to care about each other's ideas during discussion." This is 5.3: it's a community norm, not task engagement.
