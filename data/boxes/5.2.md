## Definition
This is about how the day is organized: how time gets used, how the room is set up, and how students move from one thing to the next. When these break down, learning time leaks away even when students want to work.

## Indicators
- Transitions take a long time or turn chaotic
- Instructional time is lost at the start or end of class, after recess or lunch, or while lining up
- Materials are hard to reach, with bottlenecks at the supply area, sink, or pencil sharpener
- The room layout makes it hard to see students, move around, or work in groups
- Pacing problems: activities run too long or too short, with downtime in between
- Routines for arrival, dismissal, bathroom, and materials aren't established

## Grade bands
### K-2
Transitions happen many times a day (carpet, tables, centers, lining up), and each one has to be taught and practiced. Songs, signals, and visual cues carry a lot of the load. Small bodies need room to move.
### 3-5
Students can manage multi-step transitions and materials jobs, but rotations and centers can bottleneck. Time lost at the start of work blocks adds up.
### 6-8
Bell-to-bell time and the transitions between classes matter, and the first and last five minutes often leak. Students can manage their own materials if systems exist. Room layout affects whether collaboration happens.

## Examples
- It takes forever to line up for lunch.
- Getting from the carpet to tables is chaos every single time.
- After recess it takes 15 minutes before we can start anything.
- My centers rotation is a mess. Kids don't know where to go next.
- Everyone crowds the supply table at once.
- I can't get to the back of the room because the desks are packed in.
- We never finish the lesson because we run out of time.
- The first ten minutes of class are wasted every day while they settle.
- Kids constantly ask to go to the bathroom and wander the hall.
- Cleanup takes longer than the activity.

## Near-misses
- "During independent work they talk and get up a lot." This is 5.1: they're already in the activity, so the issue is engagement with the work, not moving between activities.
- "Lining up turns into pushing and name-calling between two groups of kids." This is 5.3 if the core is conflict between kids, and 5.2 if the core is the routine itself. Go with what the teacher emphasizes.
- "They won't stop talking when I give the attention signal." This is 5.1 if it's about responding to expectations during instruction, and 5.2 if it's specifically about stopping one activity to move to the next.
