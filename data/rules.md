# How the system reads her: the line calls

These are the rules the system follows when it decides where an account sits and what the
middle of the paraphrase says. A teacher never sees these lines as written, but they decide
what she is shown, so they belong to Kim like every other word in `data/`.

- `status:` is `final` when the rules in that section are Kim's own, otherwise `DRAFT` and who wrote them.
- Each line that starts with `- ` is one rule. Add, cut or rewrite them freely.
- Keep the three headings as they are.

The prototype banner stays on the page while any section here says DRAFT.

## threshold
status: DRAFT by Luna

- Decide by what she would have to act on, never by a word that happens to appear in her account. The number of minutes the building gives her for science is beyond; how cleanup runs inside those minutes is hers. Not knowing how to teach long division is beyond; losing the rest of the room while she teaches it is hers. A guardian who never signs the planner is beyond; where that student sits is hers.
- Mixed accounts. If any part of what she wrote is hers to move, answer "inside". The next step reads only that part.
- Severity comes before the mixed rule. When she describes a violent or dangerous episode (furniture thrown, someone hurt, a room cleared or evacuated) or says the usual procedures do not touch what is happening, the account is beyond even though she was in the room and acted. Use "crisis" for an episode or a run of episodes. Use "atypical" when she is describing the standing condition of the whole room.
- The word "discipline" places nothing by itself. Teachers often use it for how severe things have become, while an office uses it for routine procedure. If she reports a label someone else put on her room, read what she says is actually happening, not the label.
- A bare topic word or phrase with no account in it ("testing", "families", "engagement", "my schedule") places nothing. Answer "inside"; the next step asks her what was happening. The same goes for a general statement that her class or her year is hard.
- How she feels in or about her own classroom is inside. Her health, pay, contract, or trouble with colleagues or administrators is beyond.
- The culture of the building, and what the adults in it do, is beyond, even though the culture inside her own room is hers.
- When she has said "mostly disagree" to a starting point, what she wrote with it is placed like anything else she writes. If it only says the starting point was off and brings no new matter, it places nothing: answer "inside".

## reading
status: DRAFT by Luna

- Most accounts fit exactly one. Add a second or third only when her words carry separate evidence for it: a different thing she is acting on, not another way of describing the same thing.
- When her account plainly carries several separate things, list each of them, strongest first. She was clear, and nothing more needs asking.
- Decide by what she is acting on. If she says one thing is fine and names another as the hard part, the hard part is the reading.
- When what she describes is about herself, and nothing in the room is named that she is acting on, none of the four fits.
- Do not use age or grade to decide anything.

## meaning
status: DRAFT by Luna

- It names what the starting place is. Example of the shape, for an account about cleanup after art running long: "the starting place is how cleanup after art is set up, the routine and where things go".
- Nothing about age or grade, even if she mentioned one. Say "students" or "they".
